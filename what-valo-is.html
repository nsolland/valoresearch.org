Canonical explanation

VALO governs the consequence, not the worker.

Agent governance governs agents. VALO governs whether an exact consequential state transition may legitimately become real under the authority and conditions that exist now.

The proposer may be an AI agent, a person, a workflow, an API, a device or another machine. The governance question remains the same.

The distinction

Runtime agent governance is useful. It is not the category VALO is limited to.

IAM, policy engines, runtime proxies, tool-call authorization, agent control planes and capability tokens can govern identities, workers, access and execution paths. VALO can use or coexist with those controls. Its primary object is different: the proposed consequence itself.

WORKER LAYER

Who or what proposes?

Agent, human, workflow, API, device or machine. Replaceable.

CONTRACT LAYER

What counts as correct completion?

Purpose, mandate, scope, constraints, evidence and completion remain authoritative independently of the worker.

CONSEQUENCE LAYER

May this exact effect become real now?

Current authority and authoritative state are resolved immediately before consequence.

The governance chain

Separate responsibilities. One governed effect path.

REHT does not govern agents. It governs whether an exact consequence may become real under the current authority and conditions. An agent-runtime control plane can sit upstream or underneath REHT without replacing that consequence-time question.
Evidence

A receipt is not merely a log entry.

The evidence should bind the proposed action, relevant authoritative state, applicable authority, the decision, what was actually released for execution, and the resulting effect or refusal. Designed intent and executed outcome remain distinct.
