VALO Research · Evidence note · 2 October 2026

Solland Paradox

Ramp datapoint: falling AI unit prices while volume rises

Main working paperRamp source

Observed

Ramp reported on 9 September 2026 that its effective price per million AI tokens had fallen 41%, from a 2026 peak of $1.15 in March to $0.68. Ramp also reported that volume had increased, while cautioning that the increase may not be large enough to offset the decline in price.

Ramp also observed that relatively cheaper standard models were driving much of the volume increase, and that some businesses were setting standard models as company-wide defaults because they remained sufficiently capable at lower cost.

What this supports

This is a relevant empirical datapoint for the demand mechanism behind the Solland Paradox: lower unit cost can coincide with expanding usage.

The mechanism is straightforward. Lower effective cost can move more tasks into the economically viable set. At the same time, individual tasks can become more intensive through additional search, verification, personalisation, agentic execution or continuous monitoring.

What it does not prove

The Ramp data does not establish physical rebound or energy backfire. Token price, compute use and electricity consumption are different measures. Falling price together with rising token volume is therefore evidence for a demand response, not evidence that aggregate physical resource consumption has increased because of efficiency.

Testable implication

The relevant empirical question is whether growth in task count and quality-adjusted service intensity together exceeds the underlying efficiency gain:

Δ ln R = Δ ln N + Δ ln I − Δ ln E

For physical backfire, the induced growth in demand must exceed the engineering efficiency improvement for the resource being measured.

Status

Demand-side mechanism: supported by current market evidence.

Physical or energy backfire: not established by this datapoint.

Next evidence requirement: identify an efficiency or effective-price shock and link it to measured compute or energy consumption against a credible counterfactual.

Source

Ara Kharazian, Ramp AI Index, 9 September 2026: Cracks in the AI thesis, part 2.
