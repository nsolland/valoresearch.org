Prepared for RETONAI · Carrier substitution

Turn carrier substitution into one bounded commercial test.

A narrow path to determine whether a useful capability can survive a change of compute or model carrier — with technical depth gated by an agreed commercial basis.

The question worth testing

Models, runtimes and hardware carriers change quickly. A durable system should not require its useful outcome to be redefined every time the underlying carrier changes.

The immediate opportunity is not a broad integration programme. It is a bounded demonstrator that asks one falsifiable question: can the same intended outcome and execution boundary survive a carrier substitution under agreed success criteria?

What we believe

Test the substitution, not the story

The useful unit of evidence is a measured before-and-after test with a locked outcome, boundary and pass/fail condition.

Separate durable outcome from replaceable carrier

The model, runtime or hardware may change. The outcome contract and consequence boundary should remain stable enough to compare the two paths directly.

Commercial basis before technical depth

A serious demonstrator should establish scope, ownership, value allocation and the decision gate before additional implementation detail is exchanged.

Proposed next step: define the commercial basis first

Agree one bounded demonstrator, the customer or workload class it represents, the baseline, the measurable success criteria and the decision that follows a pass or fail.

Before further technical material is exchanged, define the commercial basis: scope, ownership of pre-existing and newly created material, permitted use of results, value allocation and what each party contributes.

Once that gate is clear, the technical test can be specified narrowly enough to produce evidence rather than an open-ended integration effort.

Open questions

  1. What single outcome would make a carrier-substitution test commercially meaningful?
  2. Which baseline should the substituted path be compared against?
  3. What must remain invariant across the carrier change for the result to count?
  4. How should verified recurring value, if any, be allocated between the parties?
  5. What technical information is actually necessary after the commercial basis is agreed?
