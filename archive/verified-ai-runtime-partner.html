The gap

Your AI can recommend. It cannot yet prove.

AI tools building on accounting APIs already handle OCR, booking suggestions and workflow automation. What they generally do not yet have:

✗

Agent identity

No persistent, verifiable identity for the AI agent that acted.

✗

Runtime policy enforcement

No server-side policy outside the LLM that blocks prohibited actions before they execute.

✗

AI-specific approval flows

No structured human approval gate tied to the AI's action, evidence and scope.

✗

AI receipts

No signed, immutable receipt proving what the AI did, what evidence it used and who approved.

✗

Work contracts for AI agents

No defined, locked scope of what the agent may do before it starts a task.

✗

Settlement / payout control

No mechanism to count value only on verified, approved outcomes — not on activity.

These gaps are not a problem today. They become a problem when an AI recommends a ledger entry, triggers a payment or acts without a traceable authorization decision.

Integration

One integration. Runtime governance across your AI stack.

reht wraps your existing AI workflow. No model changes, no ERP modifications.

1

Define a work contract

Specify what actions the agent may take, what evidence is required and what human approval triggers. Takes minutes to configure per workflow type.

2

Route actions through the policy gate

Before your AI touches any accounting API, the action request passes through reht's policy check. Prohibited actions are blocked. Permitted actions continue. Approval-required actions wait.

3

Capture human approval

The accountant or user sees a structured review — action, evidence, confidence, impact. They approve or reject. The decision is timestamped and signed.

4

Write the receipt

Every approved action gets an immutable receipt: agent identity, work contract, evidence used, human approval, outcome. Readable by the customer, their accountant or a regulator.
