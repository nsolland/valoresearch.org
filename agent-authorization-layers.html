Agent Authorization Layers

An agent can be capable of an action without being authorised to cause it.

The layers

Agent execution crosses distinct control questions: who or what is acting; whether that identity is authenticated; what authority has been delegated; what constraints apply; and whether the exact proposed consequence is authorised now.

VALO treats these as separate concerns. Authentication establishes identity claims. Policy expresses constraints. Authority determines whether the actor has the right to cause a particular effect. A consequence-time check resolves that authority against current state immediately before the effect is committed.

Core distinctionCapability is not authority. A tool connection, credential, model decision or prior approval does not by itself establish a current right to cause an effect.

Why this matters

Long-running and autonomous workflows can outlive approvals, roles, limits and context. Separating these layers makes stale authority, revoked delegation and out-of-scope effects independently testable rather than implicit in the workflow.
