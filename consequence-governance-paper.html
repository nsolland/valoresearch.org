Consequence Governance: Governing the Transition from Proposed Action to Real-World Effect

A public, citable paper defining Consequence Governance around a single operational question: may this exact proposed consequence legitimately become real now?

Core contribution

Govern the consequence, not the worker.

The paper separates the proposer from the governed object. Models, agents, people, workflows, APIs and devices may propose actions; the governing question remains whether the exact proposed consequence is legitimate under current authority, evidence and conditions.

  • Consequence Governance — the top-level governance category.
  • Governed Effect Path — the architecture through which consequence-bearing effects must pass.
  • Consequence-time authorization — fresh exact-action authorization immediately before effect.
  • NO_DIRECT_EFFECT_PATH — the invariant prohibiting bypass of the governed effect path.
Public architecture

From proposal to evidenced consequence.

The public architecture separates evaluation, authorization, deterministic decision binding, mechanical enforcement and evidence. REHT provides consequence-time authorization semantics; RACS binds deterministic governance decisions; the Gateway/PEP controls the effect boundary; receipts preserve evidence of what was evaluated, decided and executed.

The paper deliberately describes architecture, boundaries and falsifiable properties without publishing proprietary production algorithms, internal policy resolution logic or deployment-specific implementation details.
