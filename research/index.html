Independent research & technology

Research that has to survive a test.

We investigate how intelligence becomes useful capability, how people retain authority over consequences, and how systems work in the physical world. We build, challenge and measure before making claims.

Research areas

Four connected questions.

Organized intelligence

Can capability, memory and continuity arise from organized interactions? We study silicon intelligence through bounded experiments. This is an active research question; results are not claimed here.

  • Released research and status

Human authority

What evidence and current permission must exist before an AI-driven action takes effect, and when is human review itself meaningful control? We study both as separate, testable questions.

  • Human Control Research
  • Consequence Governance
  • VAIG and admissibility
  • Formal verification

Work and economics

How can intelligence create accepted outcomes with less time, cost and waste? We examine changing software lifetimes, verification capacity and repeatable production.

  • Useware
  • Verification bandwidth
  • Portable capability
  • AI economics and infrastructure papers

Physical systems

How can existing equipment and places gain useful local intelligence without losing human control? We investigate edge execution and retrofit architectures alongside physical projects.

  • Universal Retrofit Intelligence
  • VALO Habitat
Method

Build. Break. Measure. Transfer.

A research claim becomes useful when its assumptions, tests, negative results and limits can be inspected.

  1. BuildMake the idea executable so the assumptions become visible.
  2. BreakTest changed state, counterexamples and failure paths.
  3. MeasureKeep evidence, separate observations from inference and state what remains unknown.
