Research thesis

AI → SI → DI

Intelligence may not only be something a system has. It may also be something a system becomes.

The shift

From capability to development.

Artificial intelligence is usually treated as a property of a model: train it, scale it, give it context and measure what it can do. We think that view may be incomplete.

AI — Artificial Intelligence

Intelligence as engineered capability.

SI — Silicon Intelligence

Intelligence as a real causal process implemented in a non-biological substrate.

DI — Developmental Intelligence

Intelligence as something shaped by history, interaction, environment and development.

Thesis

Intelligence is not only trained. It develops.

If development matters, the present state of a system may not be sufficient to characterize the intelligence that system has become.

Engineering implication

Not only larger general models.

A developmental view points to another possibility: many smaller specialist intelligences that develop within bounded domains, environments and histories.

Legal. HR. Finance. Engineering. Medicine. Insurance. Parsing. Research. Operations.

A general system may still coordinate, communicate and reason across domains. Much of the operational expertise may instead reside in smaller systems that become specialized over time.

Testable prediction

Equivalent beginnings. Different development.

Start with equivalent systems. Give them different developmental histories. Then place them in the same environment.

If they remain meaningfully different, development itself is part of intelligence.

If the prediction fails, the developmental thesis must be weakened.

Research boundary

A thesis, not a recipe.

This page states the research position and its falsifiable prediction at a high level. It does not disclose implementation mechanisms, internal architectures or proprietary experimental methods.

Three questions.

AI asks what a model can do. SI asks what intelligence is realized in. DI asks what intelligence can become.
