Research question · Decision intelligence · 14 November 2026

Time-Critical Decision Intelligence

Choosing the right analysis before the opportunity to act is lost.

Research direction by Njål Gaute Solland · REHT Research Group · Concept and experimental design; not a validated performance claim.

The problem

In crisis management, multinational logistics and mission preparation, an apparently optimal answer can arrive too late. Additional model reasoning, expert consultation and simulation may improve a decision—or consume scarce time without materially changing the feasible choice.

The relevant objective is therefore not simply to maximise analytical depth. It is to support the best justifiable, feasible decision available at the moment a decision must be made, under the information, resources, uncertainty, authority and deadlines that actually apply.

A decision can be well justified ex ante and still have an unfavourable outcome ex post. Equally, a poorly founded decision may succeed by chance. These must be evaluated separately.

Central research question

Can time-aware, adaptive selection of AI reasoning strategies and heterogeneous specialist models improve decision quality under uncertainty and strict deadlines compared with fixed decision-support approaches?

The investigation asks whether a system can assess the marginal value of another token, model call, specialist analysis or simulation, and choose whether to continue, challenge an assumption, present the current best option, or stop.

Proposed method

  • Time and opportunity awareness: Account for the remaining decision window, model latency, uncertainty and the cost of delayed action.
  • Adaptive specialist selection: Choose among language-model reasoning, mathematical or stochastic optimisation, predictive models and bounded simulation where their expected contribution justifies the resources.
  • Decision-grounded evaluation: Compare the feasibility, expected utility, robustness and explanation quality of recommendations—not merely linguistic fluency.
  • Human responsibility and bounded autonomy: Keep the decision authority defined by the scenario and applicable policy. Any automated action remains within a previously specified authority boundary.
  • Model agnosticism: Compare interchangeable reasoning and specialist modules rather than requiring one proprietary AI model.

Proposed BattleVerse-relevant experiment

The initial research scenario concerns multinational logistics and mission readiness. Several participating organisations share transport and sustainment dependencies. A transport asset becomes unavailable or demand changes while a decision window is closing. An operator must choose among competing resource-allocation plans using incomplete information.

The experimental method would compare (1) conventional mathematical or stochastic optimisation, (2) a strong single language model, (3) a fixed multi-model or specialist workflow and (4) adaptive selection under comparable deadlines and resource budgets. Where feasible, human-led evaluation would be analysed separately.

Potential outcome measures include decision quality at decision time, feasibility, deadline compliance, regret, latency, model cost, uncertainty calibration and performance under changed scenarios. Very short deadlines would be measured as stress conditions, not promised performance.

Scientific status and limitations

This is a proposed hypothesis, not a demonstrated improvement. Anytime algorithms, metareasoning, value of information and value of computation are established research fields. LLMs connected to optimisation or simulation are also not new by themselves.

The possible contribution is the empirically tested allocation of limited analysis time across heterogeneous specialists in a simulation-backed, time-critical decision context. Results may show that simpler methods outperform adaptive orchestration at some deadlines. Such negative findings are scientifically relevant.

The proposed research is not a production military command system and makes no claims of autonomous weapons functionality, operational validation or achieved TRL 5.

Related public engineering work

JevRouter is a publicly inspectable project concerned with capability routing, typed choices and controls around model and tool selection. It represents relevant engineering background, not proof that time-adaptive mission decision support has been built or validated.
